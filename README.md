# Residential Project Feasibility Analysis

### Atul Life Phase I | Mulund West, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

---

## Project Overview

An independent financial feasibility analysis of Atul Life Phase I, a residential development located in Mulund West, Mumbai.

This project involves building an integrated Excel-based financial model to evaluate the development's revenue potential, construction expenditure, sales absorption, customer collections, active debt servicing, and projected investment returns.

The analysis combines publicly available project disclosures, market research, and explicitly documented modelling assumptions to develop a structured, go-forward view of the project's financial feasibility.

## Financial Model

The financial model consists of ten interconnected worksheets:

| # | Worksheet | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project Overview | Project information and key feasibility metrics |
| 2 | Assumptions & Sources | Input documentation, sources, confidence levels, and scenario switches |
| 3 | Unit Inventory | Residential inventory and unit-level analysis |
| 4 | Market Research | Market benchmarks and comparable pricing |
| 5 | Revenue Model | Revenue estimation and dynamic pricing assumptions |
| 6 | Construction Costs | Construction expenditure forecasting |
| 7 | Sales & Collections | Dynamic sales absorption and customer collection schedules |
| 8 | Debt & Financing | Active construction debt waterfall, interest servicing, and 30% cash-flow sweep |
| 9 | Project Cash Flow | Go-forward project-level cash flow forecasting |
| 10 | Feasibility Summary | Go-Forward XNPV/IRR, automated integrity checks, and 2-way sensitivity matrix |

## Key Analytical Components

- Residential inventory and carpet area analysis across 2 BHK and 3 BHK configurations.
- Comparable market pricing and revenue estimation with dynamic price-adjustment controls.
- Construction cost estimation and go-forward expenditure forecasting.
- Dynamic monthly sales absorption (Base / Downside / Upside scenario toggle) and inventory tracking.
- Customer collection schedules aligned to booking velocity.
- Active debt waterfall modeling interest-before-principal servicing and a 30% excess cash-flow sweep.
- Go-forward project cash flow forecasting and XNPV / XIRR return analysis.
- Two-way sensitivity matrix stress-testing Go-Forward XNPV across price adjustments (-15% to +10%) and absorption velocity (5 to 15 units/month).
- Built-in model integrity checks verifying cash flow reconciliation and zero-balance debt payoff.

## Version 2.0 Enhancements

- **Go-Forward Valuation:** Isolated remaining go-forward cash flows from historical sunk expenditure to compute actionable project XNPV.
- **Active Debt Servicing:** Replaced static financing assumptions with an automated monthly interest-and-principal sweep waterfall.
- **Dynamic Stress-Testing:** Added a centralized scenario switch and a live 30-scenario 2-way sensitivity table on `Feasibility Summary`.

## Data Sources & Methodology

The analysis draws on publicly available MahaRERA disclosures, project documentation, market research, and supporting project information.

A structured assumptions register distinguishes between:

- Documented information.
- Reported project information.
- Preliminary market benchmarks.
- Modelling assumptions.
- Inputs requiring further research.

Where reliable information is unavailable, assumptions and limitations are explicitly identified rather than presented as verified project facts.

## Important Limitations

This is an independent analytical exercise, not an official financial statement, valuation, or feasibility assessment issued by the developer.

Certain inputs, including market asking prices, sales absorption, construction expenditure timing, financing terms, and historical cost allocations, involve assumptions or estimates.

Projected financial outcomes should therefore be interpreted in the context of the model's documented assumptions, source quality, and data limitations.
